# Success Criteria and Decision Process

## Defining Success: Beyond ROI

While return on investment calculations remain essential for business case development, sophisticated buyers like Sarah, Marcus, David, and Lisa evaluate success through specific operational outcomes that demonstrate tangible value. These outcomes must be measurable, achievable within defined timeframes, and directly connected to existing business metrics that executives already track.

**Early Operational Outcomes (0-6 months post go-live)** validate the investment through quick wins following the typical 3-6 month implementation period. Research confirms that enterprises are achieving **6-12 month ROI payback** with drone inventory solutions. Gather AI reports customers seeing **3x-5x returns on investment**, with one client cutting inventory errors by 66%. Corvus Robotics specifically advertises a **"6 month ROI payback"** for its inventory drone system—in their warehouse case study, eliminating manual counts achieved payback in half a year. Geodis saw efficiency gains after implementing drones, with **1,200 locations/day scanned** compared to 800 locations taking 4 workers a full day manually—a **15× speed improvement**. These validated results demonstrate that inventory accuracy jumping from 85% to 95% within the first quarter eliminates expedited shipping costs while safety incidents dropping to zero in counting operations removes workers from danger and reduces insurance premiums.

**Transformational Business Outcomes (6-18 months)** deliver strategic value beyond operational improvements. The ability to dynamically reorient facility layouts based on real-time demand patterns increases throughput by 20-30%. Predictive maintenance triggered by visual degradation patterns reduces equipment downtime by 40%. Automated compliance documentation saves 200+ hours monthly while improving audit scores. Work order generation from drone observations shifts 60% of maintenance from reactive to preventive. Customer self-service portals showing real-time inventory status reduce call center volume by 45%.

## The Decision Journey: From Skepticism to Advocacy

The path from initial awareness to signed contract for enterprise drone solutions follows a complex journey that requires addressing technical, operational, financial, and organizational concerns. Understanding the personality types that resist change—as outlined in "Driving Technical Change"—helps navigate these conversations more effectively.

### Common Detractor Personalities and Mitigation Strategies

**The Uninformed** lack understanding of drone capabilities and default to "it won't work here." Address them with education through site visits, demonstrations, and case studies from similar operations. Transform their ignorance into curiosity by showing specific applications to their challenges.

**The Herd** wait for others to move first, citing "we'll adopt when it's proven." Counter with references from industry peers, highlighting early adopter advantages, and showing the growing momentum in their sector. Create FOMO by demonstrating competitive advantages their peers are gaining.

**The Cynic** believes all technology promises are hype, often saying "we've heard this before." Win them over with hard data, pilot program results, and by under-promising and over-delivering. Let results speak louder than sales pitches.

**The Burned** had bad experiences with previous automation projects and carry that skepticism forward. Acknowledge their past experiences, show how drone technology differs, and offer risk-mitigation strategies like phased deployments and success-based pricing.

**The Time Crunched** are too busy fighting daily fires to evaluate new solutions. Respect their time with concise presentations focused on solving their immediate pain points. Offer to handle the heavy lifting of implementation.

**The Boss** needs their authority respected and fears being bypassed by technology. Position drones as tools that enhance their decision-making power with better data. Ensure they're visibly leading the transformation, not following it.

Beyond personality types, technical concerns about reliability in industrial environments require proven deployment examples. Organizational fears about job displacement need careful change management showing how drones augment rather than replace human capabilities. Financial decision processes demand clear ROI calculations, reference customers, and phased approaches that reduce perceived risk while building confidence in the solution's viability.
