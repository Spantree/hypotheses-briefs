# Spantree Drone AI Code of Ethics: Technology for Good

## Preamble: The Unique Responsibility of Drone Technology

Drone technology represents a profound leap in human capability—the ability to extend our reach, vision, and impact across vast distances with unprecedented autonomy. As an emerging technology operating at the intersection of artificial intelligence, robotics, and aviation, drones possess characteristics that demand exceptional ethical vigilance:

- **Dual-use potential**: The same capabilities that enable life-saving search and rescue can enable invasive surveillance
- **Force multiplication**: Drones dramatically amplify human action, making ethical choices exponentially more impactful
- **Autonomy concerns**: AI-powered drones can make decisions without human intervention, raising questions of accountability
- **Accessibility**: As costs decrease, powerful drone capabilities become available to more actors, both beneficial and harmful
- **Regulatory gaps**: Technology is advancing faster than governance frameworks, placing greater responsibility on developers

Given these unique challenges, we at Spantree recognize that drone AI companies bear a heightened ethical responsibility compared to many other technology sectors. The decisions we make today about how this technology is developed and deployed will shape its role in society for generations to come.

## Our Commitment

At Spantree, we embrace this heightened responsibility. We believe that advanced drone AI technology carries both tremendous potential for positive impact and profound obligations to society. We are committed to deploying our innovations in ways that improve lives, protect the environment, and uphold human dignity. This Code of Ethics guides our decisions about which projects to pursue and which to decline, ensuring our technology serves the greater good while acknowledging the special burden of care that comes with pioneering such powerful capabilities.

## Core Principles

### 1. **Do No Harm**

We will not knowingly deploy our technology in ways that directly cause physical, psychological, or environmental harm to individuals or communities. Given drones' capacity for remote action and surveillance, we apply this principle with exceptional rigor.

### 2. **Respect Human Rights**

We uphold fundamental human rights including privacy, freedom of expression, freedom of assembly, and protection from discrimination. The aerial perspective and data collection capabilities of drones make this principle especially critical to our work.

### 3. **Environmental Stewardship**

We prioritize applications that protect and preserve our planet, refusing projects that accelerate environmental degradation. Drones' ability to access and monitor remote environments creates both opportunities and responsibilities we take seriously.

### 4. **Transparency and Accountability**

We maintain clear documentation of our ethical decisions and remain accountable to our stakeholders and society. The autonomous nature of drone systems demands exceptional transparency about capabilities, limitations, and decision-making processes.

### 5. **Positive Impact**

We actively seek opportunities where our technology can create meaningful positive change in safety, efficiency, and quality of life. We recognize that as pioneers in this field, our choices help establish industry norms and societal expectations.

### 6. **Precautionary Principle**

Given the emerging nature of drone AI technology and its potential for unforeseen consequences, we err on the side of caution when ethical implications are unclear, choosing restraint over potentially harmful innovation.

## Prohibited Use Cases

### Military and Defense Applications

**We will NOT support:**

- Weaponized drone systems or components
- Target identification or tracking for offensive military operations
- Intelligence gathering for combat operations
- Autonomous systems designed to harm individuals
- Battlefield surveillance or reconnaissance
- Military supply chain management for weapons or ammunition

**Exception:** We may consider purely defensive applications such as:

- Search and rescue operations
- Humanitarian aid delivery in conflict zones
- Natural disaster response coordination

### Law Enforcement and Surveillance

**We will NOT support:**

- Mass surveillance of civilian populations
- Facial recognition for tracking individuals without warrant
- Monitoring of peaceful protests or political gatherings
- Predictive policing based on demographic profiling
- Border patrol surveillance targeting refugees or asylum seekers
- Social credit scoring systems

**Exception:** We may consider:

- Search and rescue operations
- Missing person investigations with appropriate oversight
- Traffic monitoring for public safety (not enforcement)
- Crowd size estimation for safety planning (not identification)

### Environmental Harm

**We will NOT support:**

- Exploration for new fossil fuel extraction sites
- Monitoring for expanded drilling or fracking operations
- Deforestation planning or illegal logging operations
- Projects that enable increased carbon emissions
- Wildlife poaching or illegal hunting operations
- Toxic waste site expansion

**Acceptable "Harm Reduction" Cases:**

- Pipeline leak detection and prevention
- Emissions monitoring for compliance
- Safety inspections of existing energy infrastructure
- Renewable energy site optimization
- Environmental cleanup coordination
- Wildlife conservation and anti-poaching efforts

### Privacy Violations

**We will NOT support:**

- Residential surveillance without explicit consent
- Employee monitoring beyond safety requirements
- Collection of personal data without transparent disclosure
- Stalking or harassment enablement
- Paparazzi or invasive media applications

## Additional Ethical Considerations

### Labor and Employment

**We will carefully evaluate:**

- **Job Displacement**: Projects that may eliminate large numbers of jobs without transition support
- **Worker Surveillance**: Excessive monitoring that violates worker dignity
- **Union Busting**: Technology used to undermine collective bargaining rights

**Mitigation Strategies:**

- Partner with clients on workforce transition planning
- Design systems that augment rather than replace workers
- Ensure transparency in workplace monitoring applications

### Agricultural Applications

**We will carefully evaluate:**

- **Pesticide Optimization**: Ensuring reduction, not increase in chemical use
- **GMO Monitoring**: Respect for organic farming boundaries
- **Water Rights**: Not enabling water theft or unsustainable usage

**Preferred Applications:**

- Precision agriculture reducing chemical inputs
- Crop health monitoring for early intervention
- Sustainable farming practice verification

### Insurance and Financial Services

**We will carefully evaluate:**

- **Discriminatory Pricing**: Using drone data to unfairly price services
- **Privacy Invasion**: Excessive property surveillance
- **Claim Denial**: Technology used primarily to deny legitimate claims

**Preferred Applications:**

- Disaster response and rapid claim processing
- Risk assessment for community resilience
- Fraud prevention protecting all stakeholders

### Real Estate and Property Management

**We will carefully evaluate:**

- **Gentrification Acceleration**: Tools that displace communities
- **Discriminatory Practices**: Enabling redlining or bias
- **Tenant Surveillance**: Invasive monitoring of residents

**Preferred Applications:**

- Building safety inspections
- Energy efficiency assessments
- Community development planning with resident input

### Critical Infrastructure

**We will carefully evaluate:**

- **Vulnerability Exposure**: Creating security risks
- **Single Points of Failure**: Over-dependence on drone systems
- **Foreign Adversary Access**: Technology transfer concerns

**Required Safeguards:**

- Robust cybersecurity measures
- Redundant systems for critical operations
- Compliance with national security guidelines

## Implementation Framework

### 1. Ethics Review Process

Every potential engagement undergoes a three-stage review:

- **Initial Screening**: Project manager assessment against prohibited list
- **Ethics Committee Review**: Complex cases reviewed by diverse committee
- **Executive Approval**: Final sign-off for borderline cases
- **Enhanced Scrutiny**: Given the unique challenges of drone technology, we apply stricter standards than typical software projects

### 2. Client Engagement Standards

- **Transparency**: Clear communication about our ethical boundaries and why they matter for drone technology
- **Education**: Help clients understand the unique ethical implications of aerial autonomous systems
- **Alternatives**: Suggest ethical alternatives when declining projects
- **End-use monitoring**: Regular verification that our technology remains deployed as intended

### 3. Continuous Monitoring

- **Regular Audits**: Annual review of all active deployments
- **Stakeholder Feedback**: Community input on our impact
- **Policy Updates**: Evolve guidelines based on emerging challenges
- **Technology Assessment**: Ongoing evaluation of new capabilities and their ethical implications

### 4. Whistleblower Protection

- **Safe Reporting**: Anonymous channels for ethical concerns
- **No Retaliation**: Protection for those raising issues
- **Investigation Process**: Fair and thorough review of concerns
